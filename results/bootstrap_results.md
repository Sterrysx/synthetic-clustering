# Bootstrap null, geometric floor, scale-free metrics, collapse cells and full-range numbers

Repository: `synthetic-clustering`, draft v4. All numbers below are read from
`results/fullrange_summary.json` (item 5) and `results/bootstrap_summary.json` (items 1-4);
the complete tables are in the appendix (generated by the `tables` stage, not typed).

## Scope and deviations

* **No reduction of data scope.** All 720 original datasets, B = 30 bootstrap resamples each
  (21,600 resamples x 2 algorithms = 43,200 rows), and RVD for **all 720,000** original-synthetic
  pairs (wall time 745 s on 16 workers; bootstrap stage 363 s).
* Wilcoxon: differences of scenario means are rounded to 1e-12 before ranking so exact ties are
  ties. With this, the v4 capped-range tests reproduce as RR W = 3294.5 (p = 0.380) and
  AG W = 1665.5 (p = 2.7e-7) instead of the manuscript's W = 3300 (p = 0.39) and W = 1665;
  the manuscript values came from float noise breaking genuine ties. All other v4
  capped-range numbers checked (Table 2, overall AG 0.864/0.823, 92.0% same outcome, partial
  eta^2, independent-pair agreement 0.816/0.772, original recovery by sigma x distribution)
  reproduce exactly.
* Choices that are definitions, not reductions: (i) within-component SD for MCD_sd = pooled
  within-**planted**-group SD per coordinate in the scaled space of the original replicate;
  (ii) collapse definitions in item 4; (iii) AG0 uses the stored k-hat_R (recomputed
  k-hat_R matched the stored value in all 720 datasets, both algorithms, both ranges).
* Extra files created besides the requested deliverables: `results/bootstrap_summary.json`
  (items 1-4, machine-readable), `results/bootstrap_results_tables.md` (generated tables), and
  checkpoint shards in `results/.partial_bootstrap_null/` and `results/.partial_relative_metrics/`
  (git-ignored; kept so a rerun resumes). No existing file was modified.
* Monte Carlo caveat: AG0 per dataset rests on 30 resamples (SE up to ~0.09 per dataset,
  ~0.04 per scenario mean). Scenario-level tests near p = 0.05 (item 1, excess km vs Ward)
  should be read as modest evidence.

**Interpretation.**
* *Does k-means reproduce k-hat more often than Ward in synthetic data (full range)?* Yes,
  overall: AG 0.839 vs 0.811, k-means higher in 90 of 144 scenarios (30 lower, 24 ties),
  W = 2088.5, p = 5.4e-5. The advantage is entirely a **Gamma** effect: Gamma 0.809 vs 0.731
  (59 vs 11 scenarios, p = 2.6e-6), significant at sigma = 0.1 (0.702 vs 0.511, p = 0.002),
  sigma = 6 (p = 0.001) and sigma = 10 (p = 0.006), not at sigma = 2 (p = 0.099). For **Normal**
  data there is no difference (0.869 vs 0.890, p = 0.595); at sigma = 0.1 Normal, Ward is
  numerically higher (0.782 vs 0.665; 18 scenarios, p = 0.136).
* *Is the CART-attributable excess different between algorithms?* Both algorithms agree with
  the original **less** often than bootstrap resamples do (excess k-means -0.032, Ward -0.016;
  both p < 1e-5 vs 0). The k-means advantage in AG is already present in the null
  (AG0 0.871 vs 0.826; Gamma 0.863 vs 0.750): k-means' k-hat is more stable under resampling.
  Beyond resampling, k-means loses **slightly more** than Ward (excess difference -0.017,
  W = 2846, p = 0.040), again only on Gamma (-0.034, p = 0.024; concentrated at sigma = 0.1,
  -0.082, p = 0.010, and sigma = 2, -0.058, p = 0.003); Normal shows no difference (p = 0.640).
  Under the capped range the excess difference is not significant (-0.009, p = 0.204;
  Gamma p = 0.151).
* Consequence for the headline: "k-means is more robust to CART synthesis" is not supported as
  a statement about synthesis. The defensible wording is that k-means' count estimate is more
  stable under resampling and therefore also under synthesis (on skewed data), while the
  agreement lost to synthesis beyond ordinary resampling is small (1.6-3.2 points overall) and
  is not smaller for k-means. The v4 sentence that a synthetic dataset "agrees with its parent
  more often than a fresh sample" should be re-anchored on AG0: synthetic agreement is below the
  bootstrap null in most scenarios (k-means below in 99 of 144, Ward in 90).

## Item 2. Geometric floor (planted k)

Floor = mean of the metric over the 30 bootstrap resamples, against the same original partition.

| metric | floor km | obs km | excess km | floor Ward | obs Ward | excess Ward |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mcd | 0.222 | 0.303 | 0.081 | 0.403 | 0.460 | 0.057 |
| mvd | 0.150 | 0.219 | 0.069 | 0.324 | 0.392 | 0.068 |
| dgini | 0.021 | 0.030 | 0.009 | 0.053 | 0.061 | 0.008 |

**Interpretation.** Ordinary resampling accounts for most of each observed metric: floor /
observed is about 0.73 (k-means) and 0.88 (Ward) for MCD, 0.69 and 0.83 for MVD, 0.71 and 0.88
for dG (ratios observed/floor 1.36, 1.14, 1.46, 1.21, 1.41, 1.14). The excess is positive in
virtually every scenario (MCD 144/144 and 143/144; MVD 144 and 141; dG 144 and 129; all
p < 1e-20), so CART adds distortion beyond resampling, but the **algorithm comparison changes**:
the v4 result that k-means has lower MCD in 133/144 and lower MVD in 144/144 scenarios is
reproduced for the observed values, but it is largely a floor difference (k-means floor lower
in 121 of 144 scenarios for MCD, 135 for MVD, 132 for dG). On the excess, k-means is **not** better:
MCD excess is larger for k-means (0.081 vs 0.057; 83 vs 61 scenarios, W = 2991, p = 8.8e-6), MVD
excess is equal (0.069 vs 0.068, p = 0.973) and dG excess is equal (0.0087 vs 0.0077, p = 0.462).
The decline of MVD with separation is mostly a decline of the floor: MVD excess is roughly flat
(k-means 0.077, 0.072, 0.076, 0.052 across sigma; Ward 0.057, 0.052, 0.098, 0.065). Gamma has
larger MVD excess than Normal (k-means 0.081 vs 0.057; Ward 0.073 vs 0.063).

**Reviewer's provisional floor.** Our floor agrees closely: overall ratios ours/reviewer
0.981-1.001, Pearson r over the 720 datasets 0.889 (dG k-means) to 0.992 (MCD Ward). The
residual differences are Monte Carlo: the reviewer's script used B = 30 (inferred from the
granularity of its dG values; the parquet does not record B), a seed `1000*rep + 7p + k` that
reuses the same resample indices across the 16 rho x sep x distribution cells sharing (p, k, rep),
and separate resample streams for k-means and Ward, whereas here seeds are unique per unit and
the two algorithms share each resample (a paired comparison). The reviewer's floor is confirmed.

## Item 3. Scale-free metrics (all 720,000 pairs)

RVD = mean_j |v_S/v_R - 1| over Hungarian-aligned clusters; MVD_pc = MVD/p; MCD_sd = MCD / s_w.

| metric | floor km | obs km | excess km | floor Ward | obs Ward | excess Ward |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rvd | 0.055 | 0.082 | 0.027 | 0.126 | 0.152 | 0.026 |
| mvd_pc | 0.026 | 0.037 | 0.011 | 0.060 | 0.070 | 0.010 |
| mcd_sd | 0.260 | 0.363 | 0.103 | 0.462 | 0.540 | 0.078 |

**Interpretation.** A synthetic cluster's within-cluster variance deviates from the original's
by 8.2% on average for k-means and 15.2% for Ward, of which 5.5 and 12.6 points are resampling;
the CART excess is about 2.7 points for both (0.027 vs 0.026, p = 0.891). Unlike absolute MVD,
the relative excess does **not** decline with separation; it grows (k-means 0.022, 0.021, 0.030,
0.035 across sigma; Ward 0.015, 0.013, 0.036, 0.041), so the v4 statement that dispersion
distortion "declines" with separation is a scale effect (cluster variances shrink in standardized
units as groups separate). RVD excess rises with k (k-means 0.019, 0.026, 0.036) and is larger for
Gamma (0.030 vs 0.024), roughly flat in p (0.028, 0.025, 0.027). MVD/p reproduces the v4 values
(k-means 0.032, 0.040, 0.039; Ward 0.067, 0.078, 0.065 at p = 2, 5, 10); its excess increases
mildly with p (k-means 0.008, 0.011, 0.013; Ward 0.006, 0.010, 0.014). MCD in within-component SD
units: CART displaces centroids by about 0.10 (k-means) and 0.08 (Ward) within-component SDs
beyond resampling (excess larger for k-means, p = 2.7e-5), rising with p (k-means 0.050, 0.099,
0.161). k-means has lower observed RVD and MVD/p in all 144 scenarios, but equal excess.

## Item 4. Collapse cells

Definitions (per algorithm and range): a dataset is **k-hat_R = 2** if its silhouette scan
selects 2; a **collapse cell** is a scenario in which >= 90% of its 5 original datasets have
k-hat_R = 2 (i.e. all 5); a **strict collapse dataset** has k-hat_R = 2 and k-hat_S = 2 in >= 90%
of its 1000 synthetic draws. Because k-hat = 2 is the correct answer when k = 2, the substantive
subset is k >= 3 (96 scenarios, 480 datasets); all-k figures are in the appendix.

Share of k >= 3 datasets with k-hat_R = 2 / share of k >= 3 scenarios that are collapse cells:

| range | algo | sep=0.1 | sep=2 | sep=6 | sep=10 | p=2 | p=5 | p=10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| full | km | 0.583 / 0.458 | 0.675 / 0.458 | 0.100 / 0.000 | 0.033 / 0.000 | 0.237 / 0.094 | 0.362 / 0.281 | 0.444 / 0.312 |
| full | hc | 0.725 / 0.500 | 0.767 / 0.583 | 0.100 / 0.000 | 0.033 / 0.000 | 0.275 / 0.094 | 0.438 / 0.312 | 0.506 / 0.406 |
| capped | km | 0.600 / 0.458 | 0.692 / 0.458 | 0.100 / 0.000 | 0.033 / 0.000 | 0.237 / 0.094 | 0.362 / 0.281 | 0.469 / 0.312 |
| capped | hc | 0.733 / 0.500 | 0.775 / 0.583 | 0.100 / 0.000 | 0.033 / 0.000 | 0.281 / 0.094 | 0.438 / 0.312 | 0.512 / 0.406 |

**Interpretation.** Full range, k >= 3: 34.8% (k-means) and 40.6% (Ward) of datasets have
k-hat_R = 2; 22 (k-means) and 26 (Ward) of the 96 k >= 3 scenarios are collapse cells; they
contain 15.3% and 18.1% of all 720,000 evaluations (strict datasets 16.5% and 16.9%), and all lie
at sigma <= 2. Inside collapse cells AG is 0.955 (k-means) and 0.929 (Ward) against 0.799 and
0.762 elsewhere, so a sixth of the design contributes near-automatic agreement. At p = 10 and
sigma <= 2 (all k), k-hat_R = 2 in 80.8% (k-means) and 90.0% (Ward) of datasets under the full
range, versus 88.3% and 92.5% capped; the v4 statement "87-90% (k-means)" is the capped value.

## Item 5. Full-range main numbers (from the stored table)

Overall RR 0.575 (k-means) / 0.579 (Ward) and AG 0.839 / 0.811, versus capped 0.585 / 0.585 and
0.864 / 0.823. The largest changes are at sigma = 0.1 (AG k-means 0.684 vs 0.751 capped; Ward
0.646 vs 0.676). RR k-means vs Ward: W = 3508.5, p = 0.750 (67 vs 53, 24 ties); AG: W = 2088.5,
p = 5.4e-5 (90 vs 30, 24 ties). Same outcome: 92.0% (RR indicator), 84.6% (AG indicator).
Independent original pairs with equal k-hat_R: 0.801 (k-means), 0.767 (Ward).
Partial eta^2 (main effects) for AG: sigma 0.245 / 0.229, rho 0.141 / 0.075, distribution
0.019 / 0.105, p 0.005 / 0.038, k < 0.001; R^2 only 0.340 / 0.349. Adding all two-way
interactions raises R^2 to 0.606 / 0.605, with large rho x sigma (0.282 / 0.179), p x sigma
(0.065 / 0.183) and sigma x distribution (0.090 / 0.090) terms, so the main-effects ranking in v4
describes about a third of the variation in AG. For RR: sigma 0.565 / 0.577, k 0.287 / 0.357,
R^2 0.633 / 0.660 (0.771 / 0.805 with interactions). At sigma <= 2 agreement depends strongly on
rho (k-means 0.546 at rho = 0 vs 0.901 at rho = 0.4; Ward 0.544 vs 0.840) and, for Ward, on p
(0.536, 0.703, 0.839 at p = 2, 5, 10: the collapse effect); at sigma = 6 the rho effect reverses slightly
(k-means 0.932 at rho = 0 vs 0.920 at rho = 0.4; Ward 0.902 vs 0.862) and AG decreases with p (k-means 0.976, 0.903, 0.899;
Ward 0.976, 0.853, 0.818).
